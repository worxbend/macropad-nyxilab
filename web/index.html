Nyxilab Macropad

Night shift
for your desk.

Twelve keys. Two screens. A stick or a knob. Zero glue.

Build one Spin it in 3D
  • 12MX keys, hand-wired, on any switch you like
  • 2IPS colour displays that redraw only what changed
  • 16addressable RGB LEDs that speak your layer's colour
  • 2 × 150 MHzRP2350 cores: one scans, one draws
  • 1 mskey scan, debounced, tap-hold aware
  • 0glue, supports or custom PCBs

Three layers.
Zero glue.

  1. Top plate. Twelve switches, both displays and the centre control, on a 10° tilt.
  2. Frame. A second colour all the way round. Print it translucent and it glows.
  3. Base. The Pico 2, two LED sticks and four rubber feet.
  4. Eight M3 screws. One length for all of them, into heat-set brass.

Two screens. No guessing.

The 1.9" display labels every key on the active layer. The 2.25" bar keeps the layer list, lock lights and a live keys-per-second graph. Only the rows that change are sent to the panel, so the pixels keep up with your hands.

Media. Play, skip, mute and brightness, with the knob on volume.

Light that means something.

Two sticks of eight WS2812B LEDs sit on the base and shine up through the frame. They take the colour of the layer you are on, so you know where you are before you look down.

Pick your centre.

Same case, same keys, same screens. Only the control in the middle changes.

KY-023 thumbstick. Mouse, scroll wheel or arrow keys, set per layer. Click it for a mouse button or Enter. Calibrates itself at every start.

KY-040 encoder and a 30 mm printed knob. Volume, undo and redo, scroll, zoom or brightness, set per layer. Press it to mute, or to switch what it turns.

Made for the work you already do.

Every key takes a keystroke, a shortcut, a media key, a mouse action or a whole macro. Layers stack them up. Here is where that pays off.

Video editing
Scrub the timeline with the knob, cut, ripple-delete and mark in and out on the keys.
Music production
Transport on one row, the knob on master volume or zoom, a layer per plugin.
Streaming
F13 to F24 are keys no app uses, so OBS scenes, mic mute and replays never collide.
Coding
Build, run, step over and step into. Macros type the snippets you are tired of typing.
Design
Undo and redo on the knob, zoom on a key, the stick as a pan tool.
CAD and 3D
The stick moves the mouse, a held key turns it into orbit. Views and tools on the rest.
Meetings
Mute, camera and leave, one press each. Tap-hold gives you push-to-talk on the same key.
Home lab
The HOST layer shows CPU, RAM, temperatures and network from your PC, and its keys run your scripts.
Gaming
Arrow keys on the stick, macros for the combos, a layer per game.
One-handed work
Modifier combos on single keys, big labels on screen, everything within a thumb's reach.

Why it is built like this.

Open, top to bottom

Parametric CAD, print files, blueprints, firmware, the host daemon and every guide live in one repository.

Parametric to the core

Change one number in params.py and the plate, frame, base, exports and drawings follow.

Checked before it prints

169 interference checks on the joystick edition, 191 on the rotary, plus clearances and overhangs, on every build.

Driverless

It is a USB keyboard, media controller and mouse at once. Plug it into anything.

Dual-core by design

Core 0 scans, debounces and talks USB. Core 1 owns the screens and the LEDs. Neither waits for the other.

Serviceable forever

Eight screws and it is open. No glue, no clips to snap, no custom board to reorder.

Signature Edition.

  1. Turn it over.
  2. The maker's mark, cut into the first layer of the base.
  3. 1.4 mm strokes, 0.4 mm deep. Sized so a 0.4 mm nozzle draws every line.

Tech specs

Keys
12 MX switches in a hand-wired 4 × 3 matrix with diodes
Centre control
KY-023 thumbstick with click, or KY-040 rotary encoder with push and a printed 30 mm knob
Main display
1.9" IPS, 170 × 320, ST7789
Bar display
2.25" IPS, 76 × 284, ST7789P3
Lighting
2 × 8 WS2812B: layer colour, breathe or rainbow
Controller
Raspberry Pi Pico 2 (RP2350, USB-C); the original Pico (RP2040) works too
USB
Keyboard, media keys and mouse in one device, plus an optional vendor interface for the host bridge
Firmware
Layers, tap-hold, macros, per-layer stick modes or knob bindings, settings saved in flash
Host bridge
Optional Linux daemon in Rust: live system stats on screen, keys that run commands
Case
Three printed layers, 159 × 98 mm, 18 to 35 mm tall, 10° tilt
Fasteners
8 × M3×8 countersunk into heat-set inserts
Underside
Engraved signature, 4 rubber feet
